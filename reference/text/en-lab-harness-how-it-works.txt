HARNESS · HOW IT WORKS
Read, understand, evolve, verify.
Four stages, shown on a process we automate: handling a customer order that arrives as an attachment. It’s the one from the Manufacturing and B2B distribution case.
OVERVIEW
HOW IT WORKS
01 · Read
We apply it to the process as it actually runs.
Not to the written procedure, which is often out of date. Harness reads the messages received, the ERP data and the files the team keeps on the side, and records what the process really does: its steps, its rules, and the exceptions someone handles by hand without it being written down anywhere.
harness — read · customer-orders
Sources read
orders@ inbox      1,240 messages
ERP                customers, items
discount grid      shared spreadsheet
Found
62  behaviours
18  business rules
9  exceptions handled by hand
02 · Understand
Every step, every rule, every exception. Visible.
What was found becomes a map: what each step does, under which conditions, and what it does when a condition isn’t met. Every behaviour is written so it can be verified, not just read.
harness — map · identify the customer
Identify the customer     step · agent
Given    an order received as PDF
When     the sender is unknown
Then     look for the customer number
in the document,
else escalate to sales admin
Linked   Match the references
Apply the customer's terms
03 · Evolve
We describe the change. Harness proposes, someone reviews.
A change is described in one sentence. Harness turns it into steps and rules, shows what it touches, and nothing goes out without approval. The example also adds a required human step: that’s where the autonomy left to agents is set, step by step.
harness — change · approval threshold
Request
Above €20,000, the sales admin
manager approves before entry.
Proposed
+ Approval threshold    condition
+ Sales admin approval  human · required
~ ERP entry             after approval
Accept  Reject
04 · Verify
17 of 19 behaviours verified. We know where the gaps are.
Every expected behaviour is checked against what the process does. The two gaps in the example are typical: manual handling absorbed them, nobody had ever written them down, and an agent would have reproduced them silently.
harness — verify · 17 / 19
✓ Customer identified (address or number)
✓ References matched to the catalogue
✓ Customer pricing terms applied
✓ Consistency checked before entry
✓ Threshold met: sales admin approval
✗ Reference not in catalogue:
no escalation path
✗ One-off discount:
rule missing from the grid
Illustrative example: the volumes, rules and gaps are built from situations observed in this industry. Not a mission we have delivered.
What is verified here is what gets measured next.
A process whose every behaviour is known is a process whose gain can be measured. For this order, the case sets three measures:
